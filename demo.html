Setting Up Your Multilingual Workspace

May 1, 2026 · 6 min read · Getting Started

A well-configured multilingual workspace is the foundation of any successful bilingual project. This guide walks you through the essential steps to get everything running smoothly from day one.

Prerequisites

Before you begin, make sure you have access to your hosting panel and a fresh WordPress installation. You'll also need to decide between WPML and Polylang as your translation management plugin — both work well, but they approach the problem differently.

Tip: If your content is primarily informational (like a knowledge base), Polylang's free tier may be all you need. For e-commerce or complex content types, WPML offers deeper integration.

Configuring RTL Support

Arabic, Hebrew, and several other languages read right-to-left. Your theme needs to handle this gracefully — not just mirroring the layout, but also adjusting spacing, punctuation placement, and numeral rendering. Most modern WordPress themes include an rtl.css stylesheet that loads automatically when the site language is set to an RTL locale.

Testing Your Setup

Once everything is configured, test both language versions thoroughly. Check that menu items are properly translated, slugs are localized, and meta tags reflect the correct language. Use the browser's developer tools to verify that the dir attribute switches correctly between ltr and rtl on the root element.
